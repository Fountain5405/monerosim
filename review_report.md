## 1. Verdict summary

Ranked by how much each would change what gets reported.

1. **The flip-back check reported in §2 and §6.4 did not check the 240 h runs.** `scripts/selfish_mining/flipback_scan.py:11` only matches timestamps of simulated day 1 (`^2000-01-01 …`), so on a 240 h run it sees the first 24 h and discards the rest (on `long_depth d3` it reports 3 reorgs on honest-001; a day-aware copy reports 38). Re-run day-aware over the 16 long runs (13 SoP, 3 stock): in the SoP runs 2 reveals were undone within 1 s and 7 within 5 s (none in the twelve 6 h share cells; the one undone switch in a stock run is ordinary longest-chain behaviour). The statements "the flip-back scan (0 undone switches outside the rejudge cells)" (results §2, line 78) and "the flip-back scan found no undone switch" (§6.4, line 278) are therefore wrong as checks. The undone reveals are not the subjective flip of the rejudge cells: all seven are OBJECTIVE decisions in which one honest block found on the displaced tip within 0.05–3.3 s made the honest branch heavier than the attacker's. In three of them the honest branch was still *shorter* (3 vs 6, 3 vs 4 and 4 vs 12 blocks; for example `fork 4426 OBJECTIVE alt 4386/3 vs main 2754/6 (nf=86) -> SWITCH`), in two it was of equal length (4 vs 4 twice, which plain cumulative difficulty would not flip), and in two it had become longer (4 vs 3, 5 vs 4, ordinary under any rule). The shorter-branch cases are exactly the behaviour albinjm's question 1 asks about, and they happen even at this network's near-zero latency.

2. **The measured reveal rate contradicts the spec's "once per 10 days" by about 30×, and the docs never say so.** The results doc measures 2.98 reveals per canonical day in the simulator and 3.13 in Model B at α = 0.33, d = 2 (§4, line 132), every one of them past the 48-object window (the attacker reveals only then, and the logged decisions are all `regime: objective`). MRL #146 says the attacker "can avoid the lateness penalties … on average once per 10 days of selfish mining … and the result will be, on average, a 3-block reorg." The repo's Model A (commit `450860c5`) reproduces the spec's *arithmetic* (0.031 % per attempt, 9.8 days to a 50 % chance), but that arithmetic is a race to 48 objects, which is not what the rule requires: the regime depends on the alt chain's `n_f` alone, and a mildly stubborn attacker reaches it ~30× more often. The docs scrutinise "about 1 per 3 years" (one attacker's rate, not a bound) but apply no such scrutiny to "once per 10 days". This is a stronger spec finding than any in §7, and it also reframes "defeats": profit is removed, but the attacker still orphans about 7 honest blocks a day at d = 2 against the spec's implied 0.3.

3. **"Defeats … at α ≤ 0.45" is not established by the simulator at α = 0.45.** The three draws are {0.220, 0.470, 0.429}, mean 0.373: one draw is above α, and the mean is one standard error below α (t ≈ −1.0, one-sided p ≈ 0.2, n = 3). The claim rests on Model B (0.356). At α = 0.40 the claim is solid (all nine draws ≤ 0.287). The summary's "0.11–0.19 at α = 0.40" is a spread of three noisy cell means that Model B puts at 0.13–0.14 for every d.

4. **The d5 reorg-depth excess has not gone away.** Pooled over all eight d5 runs (214 reveals, including the three banfix runs the doc keeps separate): mean depth 3.58 against Model B's 3.34, one-sided p ≈ 0.02 (normal approximation, sd 1.74); 10+ events 2 against 0.52 expected (P(≥2) ≈ 0.10). The doc's "did not replicate … treated as sampling variation" (§4, lines 150–158) rests on the three banlog runs alone (p = 0.59). One of the two 10-block events came 7.5 s after a ban wave, so the first d5 run is confounded. Summary row 2 ("reproduces … once replicated, reorg depth", High) and the §5 d5 rate ("once per 5 months") are less secure than stated; the p-values quoted are one-sided with the direction chosen after the fact.

5. **The re-judging story (claim 6) needs both halves to be stated.** Part (c) calls the rejudge result "a monerod accident, not a reading of the spec". The logs confirm the mechanics (32 of 32 failed re-files are the duplicate check; flips within 1 ms; honest nodes never saw the branch). But the flip also needs the spec's *formal* `n_f` (alt-chain objects only): with the honest block as alt, `n_f` = 12-ish, the regime is subjective, and the attacker's 6 main blocks weigh 6 units. Under the issue's own prose, "the two chains differ by fewer than k·w work objects", the pair would be objective (the attacker holds ≥ 48) and no flip could occur. So the result is "monerod's one-at-a-time re-insertion × the formal n_f definition", and item 1 above shows whole-branch mode is not immune to reversals either.

6. **Superseded SoP numbers still stand unmarked in three places**, and the main docs disagree on small facts: `docs/20260926_dev_branch_handoff.md` (lines 162–167, 288–290, 310–311: {0.035, 0.000}, {0.440, 0.326}, "2,822 of 2,822", {0.405, 0.495}, "SoP 0.000 in all four cells") carries no notice; `docs/20260925_e4_code_data_review.md:215` likewise; `docs/SELFISH_MINING.md:801` still calls Share-or-Perish "planned". The manuscript says "10 runs of 240 h" (line 460) where the results doc says 13; both call `window_stubborn` "the spec's own stubborn attacker" while results §5 says the two attackers "differ in more than the give-up rule"; results §8 cites a "§7.2" that does not exist; the §2 table labels the 2026-09-30 stock rows "pop `2d6499a8`" although their provenance files record pop patch `899cd283…` (the pre-fix build; harmless for stock cells, as argued below).

7. **Run independence is assumed.** The five d5 runs use seeds 12345 (twice), 54321 (twice) and 24680; bans occurred in both 12345 runs (48, 10), neither 54321 run (0, 0) and the 24680 run (57). Whether the Shadow seed fixes topology placement or latencies cannot be checked from the extract (`topology.gml` is not included). The doc treats the runs as fresh draws on the author's word (commit `487499f2`: "Runs are not seed-reproducible").

8. **Model B at α = 0.45 predicts 10+ reorgs daily, which no document mentions.** A 400-day Model B sweep (seed 11) gives 181 reorgs of 10+ blocks per 261 canonical days at α = 0.45, d = 2 (253 per year), beside the attacker's 0.356 share. The headline "defeats … up to α = 0.45" is a profit statement only; the depth side at that α is unreported.

**What is solid.** Every number in the results doc that I could trace reproduces from the stored data and the logs: the §3 revenue table and ranges from the 69 cell records; the §4 Model B table, both depth tests and the §6 ban replay from the committed scripts, byte-identical to the stored outputs; the oracle gives 0 mismatches over 43,949 logged decisions in 37 runs; the ban mechanism, its constants and the fix are exactly as described in v0.18.5.1 and the patch; the hard-fork reading of `version_minor` is correct; the attacker code does what §2 says; the attacker never embeds a remote share in any attack run; the `has old version` rejections, the 48 bans in three waves, relay-002's 13.7 h isolation and 24 h resync, and the two 10-block events are all in the logs where the doc says they are.

## 3. Method critique

**Topology.** Twelve daemons on a sampled CAIDA graph, two equal honest miners, one attacker bridge, six seeds, two relays (`test_configs/selfish_micro_sop.yaml`). Propagation is close to instantaneous: in the seven reversed reveals the honest block that flipped the network arrived 0.05–3.3 s after the reveal. Two effects follow. Honest blocks embed 96 % of honest shares (Model B: 98.5 %), which is close to the best case for honest weight; albinjm reports 15–16 % share loss at a 3 s mean delay. And reversals of a reveal are rare here (2 % of reveals, "shorter but heavier" ones 1 %) only because honest miners switch within a second; with real delay the window in which a honest block lands on the old tip is seconds, not milliseconds. The results doc's §9 covers scale in general terms but not this mechanism. The h10 snapshot (994 blocks, `monerosim.log`) gives every node `h0 = 994` (patch L1179), so `h0 < h_f` always holds and the subjective regime is always reachable; that is appropriate. The base config comment still says "336 pre-mined blocks" (`selfish_micro_sop.yaml:18`), a stale remark.

**Statistics.** The 240 h α = 0.33 results are solid: 13 runs, each with 20–34 reveals, all shares ≤ 0.025. The 6 h cells are not: each has 1–9 reveals, so a cell's share is a few events, and n = 3 draws give standard errors of 0.05–0.08. Ranges of means ("0.11–0.19") are presented where a mean and error would be honest. The depth tests are resampling tests against Model B's exact distribution and reproduce; but the p-values are one-sided in the direction observed, with three configurations tested, and the "replication" verdict uses one subset (banlog) while the other subsets (long_depth, banfix) and the pool all read deep. The two 10-block events are judged against Model B with one of them 7.5 s after a ban wave. Nothing is wrong with the arithmetic; the inference "sampling variation" is a choice the data do not force.

**Model assumptions (Model B).** Fixed difficulty and a fixed object rate: the simulator's difficulty fell about a third under the attack (summary of the d2 run: final difficulty 803 from 1,200), so per-time rates were converted to canonical days (commit `e1b6b9db`); per-1000-found rates sidestep this and agree (2.81 vs 2.95). No latency: see above. Honest pending shares are always embedded in the next honest block, and the attacker embeds only its own. No honest-vs-honest forks (the simulator has 2–12 "other reorgs" per 240 h run, median 9). The attacker's private chain has the main chain's difficulty. These are reasonable for a first model, and the agreement on revenue, reveal rate and race shapes is genuine; the depth discrepancies are the place where one of these assumptions may bite, and the doc does not try to say which.

**Attacker fidelity.** `window_stubborn` is coded as described and is a sensible "reveal at the first objective chance, give up at d blocks behind" attacker. It is not the spec's attacker in two respects the docs only partly acknowledge: tevador's T5 counts the give-up in work objects and only reveals when it can orphan 10+ blocks (`blockhain-sim.py:109–116`), and tevador's attacker uses honest shares in its first private block (`:70–80`), which his first comment calls the reason 10+ reorgs are possible at all ("If the attacker doesn't include honest workshares in his first private block, achieving a 10+ reorg is nearly impossible"). Ours never does (verified in every attack run). So the headline is about a weaker attacker on the deep-reorg axis and a different one on the give-up axis; results §9 says the first, the summary row says neither.

**Simulator fidelity to the spec.** Tables 3–5 are implemented as written (ledger 1.c), the conventions are documented and the oracle is a genuinely independent re-derivation (it reads only the logged inputs and was written from the doc; it agrees on 43,949 decisions). Points to keep in view: (i) the `n_f` deviation (shares shared with the main block at the fork height are counted) is irrelevant for attack forks here but would matter in a many-pool network; (ii) the `version_minor` slot rule is enforced at template time by first-seen-per-slot (patch L343–391) and at weight time by a whole-set check (L917–944); a violating set counts as the header alone, which is lenient relative to the issue's "requires"; (iii) the share pool is capped at 1,024 entries (L683–690), harmless at 12 nodes; (iv) exact-tie draws are random per pair, as the spec says.

**Confounds.** (1) The ban cascade: three of five d5 runs, and the first d5 run's 10-block event, are not "SoP as specified on a healthy P2P layer". The banfix runs are the clean d5 sample. (2) Shadow v0.2.5 for banfix only, unpinned CPUs from 2026-10-04 15:57: acknowledged; the attack-rate comparison (2.82 vs 2.18 per 1,000, n = 3 each) cannot see an effect of less than ~30 %. (3) Seeds: three seeds reused across matrices; two of the five d5 runs share a seed with another; bans clustered by seed. Not verifiable from the extract. (4) The 2026-09-30 stock rows ran on the pre-fix binary (`899cd283`); the stale-id path needs `sim-sop-w > 1`, so this is a provenance error in the table rather than a result risk.

**Analysis scripts.** Where I spot-checked them against raw lines they are right: reveal counts equal my own count of objective SWITCH decisions on honest-001 (23 in the d2 run), ban counts equal a plain grep, the replay's accounting mirrors `add_host_fail`, and all stored outputs reproduce. The exception is the flip-back scanner's day-1 timestamp pattern (ledger A1), which also makes its "honest flip pair" and "failed-push" columns cover one day of each long run.

## 4. Interpretation critique

- **"Defeats."** What is shown is that this attacker's revenue share falls far below α at α ≤ 0.40 and that honest miners lose about 1 % of their blocks instead of 17 % (manuscript finding 9, verified from the reveal depths: 198 honest blocks orphaned of 20,777 found under SoP d2; 3,061 of 17,990 under stock). What is not shown is that the attack is rare: it succeeds about three times a day at α = 0.33, d = 2, against the spec's "once per 10 days". Both facts belong in the same sentence.

- **The 48-object window is easier to pass than the spec's analysis assumes.** The rule asks for `n_f ≥ k·w` on the alt chain plus more weight; the spec's probability asks for 48 attacker objects before 48 honest objects. Model A reproducing the spec's arithmetic (commit `450860c5`) is a check of the arithmetic, not of the claim. The simulator and Model B agree that the claim is off by ~30× for a d = 2 attacker, and the result gets stronger with d. This is the most important thing the owner could tell the thread that is not in it.

- **α = 0.45.** The doc says "defeats … at α ≤ 0.45" with "Moderate" confidence; the data support "below α at 0.40; undetermined at 0.45 (Model B says 0.36)". Model B also says the 10+ reorg rate at α = 0.45 is hundreds per year; the manuscript's "its margin shrinks with α" understates what the model predicts at the top of the range.

- **Reorg depth.** The honest reading of the d5 data is "the simulator's reveals are a little deeper than Model B's, consistently across the long_depth, banfix and pooled samples, marginally significant, with one ban-confounded run; cause unknown". The doc's "did not replicate" is one admissible reading, chosen in the direction of the model.

- **Re-judging.** The four-part position is accurate on mechanics (ledger 6.a–6.d) but the conclusion "a monerod accident, not a reading of the spec" should be "an interaction of monerod's re-insertion with the spec's formal `n_f`". Two further points: the issue's prose sentence supports a symmetric reading under which neither the rejudge flips nor the subjective judging of a long main branch can occur; and whole-branch mode reverses reveals too, through a different and legitimate route (in three of seven cases a shorter honest branch out-weighing a longer attacker branch objectively, which is precisely albinjm's question 1). Both readings should be put to the thread rather than one being adopted as "the" reading.

- **Hard fork.** Correct for the rule as written: any block with fewer than 16 workshares (including zero) carries a vote below 16 and is rejected by v0.18 (`get_block_vote`, `do_check`). It is also trivially repairable (another field, or an offset encoding), so it is a drafting defect in the proposal rather than a structural one; worth saying when reporting it.

- **Ban cascade.** Real, fully explained, and specific to the interaction of weight-based fork choice with height-based peer scoring. The fix is a blunt sim-only instrument; the doc says so. What is not said is that any deployment would also need to look at the other places where monero treats a falling height as evidence of misbehaviour or stalls syncing on it (not checked here).

- **"One attacker's rate, not a bound."** Correct for "1 per 3 years". The same sentence applies to the doc's own Model B rates: they are one attacker's rates under a no-latency model whose deep tail the simulator cannot confirm.
