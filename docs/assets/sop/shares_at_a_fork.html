What it does to the deep-reorg rate

tevador's MRL #146 post estimates about one reorg of 10 or more blocks every 3 years, for an attacker with a third of the hashrate. Model B (scripts/sop_race_model.py) gave about one every 21 years for a similar attacker. Switching his attacker's differences on one at a time shows where the gap comes from. In every row the attacker has α = 1/3, gives up when 5 work objects behind, and reveals once the honest branch is 10 blocks deep. Model B rows pool two seeds of 40,000 days each.

  1. Restarting level: after giving up, the attacker adopts the honest tip. The honest shares already found on that tip are public, so its template carries them from the start.
  2. The honest shares on the fork point give the next factor of two. They sit in both rival first blocks, so they stop counting as an honest lead (act 2).
  3. The window and tie rules change almost nothing at this depth: by the time the honest branch is 10 blocks deep, the window has long passed.
  4. Model B's tevador row and his script agree within their 95% ranges, 2.2–3.8 and 1.3–3.7 years.
  5. The last row needs two shares in one slot (act 3), so no attacker can reach it. It only checks the mechanics: his script with UNIQUE_SHARES = False gives an attack every 11.1 days, and Model B every 10.8.

Our daemon attacker uses no honest shares

In the monerod simulation the attacker's miner runs with --offline (test_configs/selfish_micro_sop.yaml:91). A node adds a share to its pool only when its own miner finds it or when the share arrives by gossip. Blocks handed to it do not add their shares (patches/monero-sim-pop.patch:645 and its two callers). In the deep_hunt smoke run, the attacker's miner built 1,199 templates and embedded none of the honest shares. Each honest node's templates were about half gossiped shares, and those match the other honest node's own count, so the attacker's shares never reached them either.

So in share handling our daemon attacker is Model B as built: its first block carries only its own shares, and it restarts with none after giving up. That makes Model B as built the like-for-like model for the daemon runs. The table gives both versions at the daemon runs' settings: give up 2 blocks behind, and reveal once the honest branch is 10 blocks deep.

  2. The honest shares matter less as α grows: a stronger attacker wins often without the head start, so it adds proportionally less. At the object give-up tevador uses (5 behind) the ratio is larger, 2.5× at α 0.40 and 1.7× at α 0.45.
  3. The daemon runs now going (α 0.45 for 72 h, α 0.40 for 240 h) should be read against the as-built column. They are too short to tell the columns apart: at α 0.45, over 2.7 canonical days, about 14 reorgs against 17.
  4. A daemon attacker that used honest shares would need some way to hand gossiped shares to its offline miner, because blocks do not carry the shares still pending on their tip.
