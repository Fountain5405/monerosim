MRL #146 Briefing

SoP (Tables 3–5, version_minor slot rule, w = 16, k = 3, d = 5 s) in monerod v0.18.5.1 under the Shadow network simulator: 12 daemons, real RandomX, 120 s blocks. Every fork-choice decision is logged and re-derived by an independent checker: 43,949 decisions, 0 mismatches. The attacker withholds, reveals once its branch holds 48 work objects and outweighs the honest chain, and gives up when honest leads by d blocks.

Both settled by the revision

Two questions we had

Q1 · Which n_f is intended?

The earlier text gave two rules that are not the same:

Formal: n_f counts work objects in the alt chain "not contained in the main chain".
Prose: the lateness factors apply only if "the two chains differ by fewer than k*w work objects".

Campaign 7 implemented the formal reading. Settled: the revision puts the prose reading into the definition, as max of the two chains.

Q2 · What happens to blocks displaced by a reorg?

After a reorg, stock monerod pushes every displaced block back through the fork choice (blockchain.cpp:1163–1180). Under cumulative difficulty that check can never win. With SoP weights it can, if the weights depend on which chain the node is on. Two things made them depend on it: the alt-only n_f, and lateness timed against whichever block was main at the time.

Settled: the revision removes both. n_f is symmetric, and main_seen is fixed per height.

Why it mattered: one pair, both directions say SWITCH

Under the earlier text, here is a case where both directions say SWITCH. An honest block H has 11 workshares, all gossiped early. Against it are 7 attacker blocks A1–A7, each with 8 workshares that were never gossiped.

Node is onAlt branchRegimeComparisonResult
A1–A7H (12 objects)subjective912 vs 532SWITCH to H
HA1–A7 (63 objects)objective63 vs 12 objectsSWITCH to A

We saw this live in a log: alt 4234/6 vs main 3796/4 → SWITCH, then 1 ms later alt 1022/1 vs main 438/6 → SWITCH. Under the revised n_f, the attacker's 63 objects make the pair objective from both sides, so it doesn't flip.

Separately, the simulator judges a displaced branch again only when a new block extends it. Reveals can still be reversed that way: 7 of 367 reveals in the 240 h runs (2 %) were reversed within 3.3 s. Each was reversed by one honest block that made the honest branch heavier, and in 3 of the 7 the honest branch was still the shorter one. That is albinjm's question 1, which the author says is intended.

Numbers not to quote. In runs where monerod re-checked displaced blocks, the attacker's share came out near zero. That is an accident of monerod's code: the switch back runs inside the first switch, the duplicate check (:2073) refuses to store the attacker's blocks again, and the outer switch deletes them (:1182–1186). It says nothing about SoP.

version_minorsoft-fork claim · resolved

Under the earlier rule, version_minor equalled the workshare count. v0.18 uses that field as the hard-fork vote and requires it to be at least 16 (hardfork.cpp:109–113). Honest blocks carry about 15 workshares on average, with a median of 11. An unmodified monerod on the same chain rejected every SoP block it was sent: 10 of 10, each with at most 7 workshares. It logged "has old version: 16, current: 16" (blockchain.cpp:4079) and dropped the peer.

The revised rule numbers shares from version_major. Under it, the same setup accepted a SoP block with 9 workshares, and no block failed the vote check (2026-10-15).

Peer bansstock P2P scoring

SoP nodes legitimately switch to a branch with fewer blocks. Stock monerod scores a peer whose reported height drops (hit_score), and the fail score never decays, so honest nodes ban each other for 24 h.

With a depth-5 attacker: bans in 3 of 5 runs (48, 57 and 10), and one relay was stuck on a stale branch for 13.7 simulated hours. Replaying the fail score from the logs predicts all 67 bans. Not scoring height drops gave 0 bans in three 240 h re-runs. That fix is blunt, and a deployment would want a narrower rule. The revision doesn't touch this, and the thread hasn't discussed it.

Limits

  • 12 daemons, two honest miners, fast propagation. Realistic delay and many pools are not tested. A 40-daemon network is running now.
  • One attacker design. It is not tevador's T5 attacker, which gives up when it falls a set number of work objects behind and uses honest workshares in its first private block.
  • α = 0.40 rests on 6 h runs. α = 0.45 is not established: three 6 h runs gave 0.22, 0.47 and 0.43, too noisy to tell apart from α (a race model gives 0.36).
  • Three seeds, and we haven't verified that a seed fixes network placement. The ban fix is untested at scale.
  • So far, the revised rules have been run only in 6 h smoke runs and one stock-node check.
