## The owner's deep-reorg question: answer and proposed experiment (NOT approved yet)

- **Answer:** not run. Every attacker here and in campaign 7 is revenue-seeking:
  - `window_stubborn` reveals at the first chance (48+ objects and heavier) and gives up d blocks behind;
  - campaign 7 §5 has Model B's 10+ rates for it (α 0.33: 0.35/0.68/2.44 a year at d2/3/5; α 0.40: 17-42 a year; α 0.45: 143-361 a year);
  - tevador's simulator has the deep-reorg hunter (abandons at more than 5 objects behind) → "about 1 per 3 years" at α 0.33;
  - nobody has run a deep-reorg hunter against STOCK, so the comparison the owner asks about doesn't exist.
- **Why SoP doesn't stop deep forks by design:** the lateness penalty applies only while n_f < k·w = 48 objects (about 3 blocks). A 10-block fork is judged on plain weight (share = header = diff/w). Any protection comes from weight granularity, and only shares carried in blocks count. That's a reading of the rule, not measured. Against more than 50 % nothing helps.
- **Proposed design** (bounded change to existing code; needs approval):
  - A new strategy `deep_hunt` in `agents/selfish_strategy.py` beside `window_stubborn` (`:217-253`).
    - It withholds until the honest branch past the fork holds at least D blocks (attribute `min_honest_depth`, default 10) AND the private branch outweighs it under the honest nodes' rule (`weigh: sop|difficulty`).
    - It abandons once honest leads by more than G (`give_up_objects`, as in Model B `sop_race_model.py:254`; or `give_up_depth` in blocks).
  - The same mode in Model B for the cells too rare for the simulator.
  - Cells: α {0.33, 0.40, 0.45} × {stock, SoP} × G {5 objects (tevador), 2 blocks}, 12 daemons, 240 h, and Model B for rates below about 1 per 10 days. Measure 10+ orphaning reorgs per canonical day and the attacker's cost (share).
  - Tests: unit tests on the strategy's hold/reveal/abandon decisions, following the existing `window_stubborn` tests.
